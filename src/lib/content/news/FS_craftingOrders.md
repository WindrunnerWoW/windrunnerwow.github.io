### What is a Crafting Order

Well, now that is pretty easy to answer:

*1. You go to a trainer*
*2. You ask him to craft for you.*
*3. You wait 15 minutes and ??? PROFIT.*

It is that simple. Of course he is no magician... or maybe he is... but there are limits, okay?

### How to use them

Every **primary** profession trainer will do this. This is crucial, since crafting is already kinda boring, but having to do Engineering, Blacksmithing and Juwelcrafting? No thanks. 

All it takes is the mats and materials. You give it to him, 15 minutes later you will get a new mail with whatever you ordered.

### How to learn more Recipes

When finding recipes in the world, you can take them to a trainer of the profession and hand it in. This will unlock the recipe for your **Account** and will allow all profession trainers for this profession to craft this item.

## Background

I think we all love having crafted items, but actually crafting them yourself can be pretty tough,especially when you’re the only one on the server. Sure, the auction house exists, and sometimes you might get lucky and find what you want there. But a lot of times, you might have all the mats, but you just don’t have the right profession. Normally, you’d just poke a guild mate or some random in trade chat for help, but obviously that’s not an option here, since it’s a singleplayer experience.

So, I started thinking about how to blend the old system with something Blizzard actually introduced,kind of creating a hybrid. In retail, you might know about crafting orders: you set one up, and another player fulfills it. Naturally, retail makes it a bit more complex. There are different quality tiers (like, five!), various item levels, and you can customize lots of things, like stats. The more details you want, the harder (and pricier) it gets. Thank god we play vanila here!

My idea was to let trainers craft stuff for you. As long as you have the materials (and a bit of gold), you shouldn’t have to grind up every profession yourself. Plus, you can disenchant stuff even if you’re not an enchanter,for a fee, of course. That means if you get loot in a raid that you don’t need, you can still disenchant it without ever touching the profession. You’re not forced into learning disenchanting, though obviously this kind of change comes with its own set of problems, which,hopefully, Ive got covered too.

In the next feature spotlight I will share a bit more about material storage, which solves the bag problem you will get in the long run. But there’s another problem: gathering all those mats. Normally, you’d have to either farm everything yourself, buy it from the AH, or pick up two gathering professions just to cover your bases,which really limits your other profession choices. Like, if you’re a leather or mail class, you’d need skinning; if you’re plate, it’s mining. Then, of course, herbalism, regardless of class. So only some classes could really pick up a “real” second profession. My change? All the gathering professions,skinning, herbalism, and mining,are now secondary professions. This way, you can learn two **real** professions, and you’re not locked into gathering if you just want to focus on other skills.

Personally, I’ve always felt gathering would make more sense as a secondary anyway, though I get what Blizzard was aiming for,having to make meaningful choices. Do you go all-in on self-sufficiency, or try to make gold through crafting? Do you grind to get materials to sell? On this kind of server, limiting that just feels bad. So my solution is: let everyone pick up any gathering profession they want. That way, people can actually use the crafting system however they like.

Of course, there were a bunch of details I had to sort out—like, what exactly should trainers be able to craft for you? What do you actually do with all those random recipes you find while leveling up? Here’s what I landed on: trainers can only craft the items they’d normally be able to teach you. So if your tailoring trainer caps at 150, that’s the highest level of tailoring crafts they can handle. 

But here’s the neat part: any recipe you come across out in the world... yes, even those awkward drops you’d normally have to keep stashed away until you felt like grinding out the skill... you can hand in to your trainer. Once you turn in a recipe, it unlocks for *all* trainers (of that profession) across your whole account. Now, every character can order that item from their local trainer, but you’ll have to pay a little fee each time you get something crafted. Classic convenience, with just a touch of gold sink.

Of course, you might be a purist and want your main crafter to learn those recipes directly. That’s still totally possible! Just keep the recipe for yourself, learn it, and enjoy the flexibility. Though you might have to juggle materials between your characters. If it’s not something you can craft or trade yourself, handing it in to a trainer is often a good option. Bottom line: if you want an item available for every character, hand in those recipes and let the trainers do the work, everywhere, account-wide. No more recipe hoarding or feeling locked in, yu choose how you want to unlock and share your collection.

So friends, talk to your neighbourhood trainers and get them to craft stuff for you!
